Provenance

Global risks report [22]; supervisory radar [27]; hazard profiles [24]; register series [9]

Specification, origin and what it reconciles

What it requires. Identification consults four controlled lists and records the match where one exists: the thirty-three global risks in five categories, scored on two horizons; the persistent and yearly vulnerabilities that supervisors publish; the dictionary of two hundred and eighty-one coded hazards; and the four-part security scenario of assets, threats, vulnerabilities and impact. A risk that matches a list carries the list's identifier as a field. The lists are mapped to the taxonomy, not adopted as it. An internal perception survey follows the published method: a defined window, a count of valid responses, a severity scale on two horizons and a rule for drawing interconnections, each recorded so that the exercise can be repeated.

Where it comes from. The global risks report [22] fixes a list of thirty-three risks in five categories, asks a surveyed community to score each on a seven-point severity scale over two years and ten years, and publishes the method. The supervisory radar [27] lists the system's persistent vulnerabilities each year and, for artificial intelligence, five specific ones. The hazard profiles [24] give each of two hundred and eighty-one hazards in eight types a persistent identifier, a definition and its metrics. The register series [9] decomposes a scenario into assets, threat sources, threat events, vulnerabilities and impacts.

What it reconciles. Chapter 11 decided that these lists are vocabularies an organization maps its taxonomy to, not the taxonomy itself, and C2.5 is that decision in the form of fields. The four lists serve four readers: the board, which has already read the first; the supervisor, who wrote the second; anyone with a dataset about a place, who uses the third; the security function, which thinks in the fourth. One register speaks to all four without adopting any of their structures, and a hazard enters it by code rather than by heading, which chapter 13 argued is the difference between a record that can leave the building and one that cannot.
