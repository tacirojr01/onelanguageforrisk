Layer L5

Monitor

Whether reality agrees with the estimate, and how fast the organisation learns. 7 components, chapter 18 of the book.

C5.1

Risk data discipline

Every number that reaches a risk report is accurate and reconciled, complete across entities and risk types, timely including in a crisis, adaptable to unplanned questions, and carries lineage: source, transformation and owner, walkable backward. Applies to the register as much as to a capital ratio.

Source: BCBS 239 [17]

C5.2

Indicators

Every indicator has a threshold; every breach and its escalation are traceable to a recipient and an action; leading indicators are reported apart from lagging ones, never mixed in one view. Threshold is distinct from limit and tolerance.

Source: BCBS PSMOR [17]; risk-engineering.org [26]

C5.3

Events, near misses and root cause

Losses and near misses enter one base with cause in a fixed taxonomy; barrier analysis names from the register the control that should have prevented and the one that should have detected the event, and why each did not; corrective action is systemic; a recurrence chart is kept until closure is demonstrated. Failed controls get their measured effectiveness and ratings revised.

Source: ASQ RCA and 8D [14]; BCBS PSMOR [17]

C5.4

Common incident schema

One structured incident record compatible with the supervisory exchange format, four sections and three phases; loss base, cyber log and third-party incidents are views of it; barrier that failed and recurrence status are extensions that keep the base record valid.

Source: FSB FIRE [27]

C5.5

Calibration

Estimates are confronted with outcomes: likelihood bands against event counts, detection ratings against escapes, simulated distributions against losses; scales and models are recalibrated with the evidence and every change is versioned so trends survive it.

Source: PMI library [12]; BIS backtesting [5]

C5.6

Continuity

A business impact analysis per process with maximum tolerable outage, recovery time and recovery point; the resource inherits the most demanding function's recovery time and the recovery point dictates backup frequency, both written as tests; exercises produce corrective actions tracked to closure.

Source: Ready.gov [16]; NIST IR 8286D [9]

C5.7

Lifecycle and triggers

Every register object has a lifecycle state with continuous authorization and an explicit disposal step; substantial change triggers reassessment and reopens the design gate; early warning runs on at least three cadences and, for physical hazards, four pillars.

Source: NIST SP 800-37 [20]; COSO principle 15 [2]; INFORM [7]; UNDRR [24]
