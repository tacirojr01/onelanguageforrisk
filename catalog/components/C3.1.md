# C3.1 · The scenario triplet

**Layer:** L3 Analyze
**Catalog version:** 1.0
**Chapter:** 16 of *One Language for Risk*
**Provenance:** Risk management handbook [11]

## What it requires

Every analyzed risk is recorded as a scenario, a likelihood and a consequence, each with its uncertainty; likelihood is a probability, a frequency or a calibrated word, never a bare adjective; consequence is stated per objective; a margin is reserved for unknown risks.

## Specification, origin and what it reconciles

What it requires. Every analyzed risk is a triplet: the scenario from C2.2, a likelihood and a consequence, each carrying its uncertainty. Likelihood is recorded as probability or frequency where a number exists and as a calibrated word where it does not, never as a bare adjective. Consequence is stated against each objective it affects. A point estimate without a stated uncertainty is incomplete, and a margin is reserved for risks unknown or underappreciated rather than pretending the register is exhaustive.

Where it comes from. The handbook [11] defines risk, for analysis, as the set of triplets of scenario, likelihood and consequence, and carries uncertainty on each rather than as an afterthought. It reserves a margin for the unknown, the most honest sentence about uncertainty in the set, and reports the aggregated risk of an alternative as the likelihood that an objective is missed.

What it reconciles. The triplet is the shape into which every other L3 method fits. A matrix cell is a triplet with its uncertainty suppressed; a simulation is a triplet with likelihood and consequence drawn as distributions; a composite index is a triplet with the consequence replaced by a score. Making the triplet the required form of the record lets the methods differ while the record does not, so that one register holds a workshop estimate and a Monte Carlo result side by side and still says which is which.
