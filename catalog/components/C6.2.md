# C6.2 · Assurance map and reliance

**Layer:** L6 Record, Assure and Report
**Catalog version:** 1.0
**Chapter:** 18 of *One Language for Risk*
**Provenance:** IIA standards 9.5 and statements [18]; Orange Book [4]

## What it requires

Every principal risk shows the assurance it receives by line and provider; reliance on another provider is assessed and documented before it is taken; the control object carries designer and assurer, and the two may not be the same person within twelve months.

## Specification, origin and what it reconciles

The second component requires that every principal risk show the assurance it receives, by line and by provider; that reliance on another provider's work be assessed and documented before it is taken, in the manner of standard 9.5 of the internal audit standards [18]; and that whoever designed a control not assure it for twelve months, the temporal safeguard the same standards apply when internal audit takes on second-line work. The assurance map itself is the device the public-sector guidance puts behind its principal-risk report [4], and it is the only source in the set that puts assurance on the same page as the risk.

What this reconciles is the three lines with one framework. A shared register, a shared taxonomy and shared indicators are exactly what the integrated-assurance literature asks for, and they create the risk the prohibitions of C0.3 guard against: that the second line ends up auditing itself, or that internal audit relies on a test it has not looked at. The map answers, for each principal risk, who vouches for what and how independent they are, and the reliance field records the assessment that justified taking another party's work. The twelve-month rule is written on the control object as two fields, designer and assurer, and a register query can find every control whose two fields name the same person within the year.
